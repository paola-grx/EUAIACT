# Core module: working safely with AI

This module is for everyone who uses, builds, oversees or decides with AI
systems on behalf of the organisation. It takes about 30 minutes.

> **How your path is tailored.** Your learning path was generated from your
> role, your background and the AI systems you work with. If something looks
> wrong, tell your AI literacy coordinator.

## 1. What AI is (and is not)

An *AI system* in the sense of the EU AI Act is a machine-based system that
infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as
predictions, content, recommendations or decisions. In practice this covers:

- **Generative AI**: chatbots, copilots, image and text generators.
- **Predictive models**: scoring, forecasting, classification, ranking.
- **Recognition systems**: speech-to-text, image recognition, document extraction.

AI systems do not "understand" in the human sense. They produce outputs that
are statistically likely given their training data and the input.

## 2. Capabilities and limits

| AI is often good at | AI is often weak at |
|---|---|
| Drafting, summarising, translating | Facts it was not trained on or cannot look up |
| Finding patterns in large data | Explaining *why* it produced an output |
| Repetitive classification | Rare cases, edge cases, changed circumstances |
| Speed and scale | Knowing when it is wrong |

## 3. Hallucinations

Generative AI can produce text that is fluent, confident and **false**:
invented facts, figures, quotes, case law or citations. Treat every factual
claim as unverified until checked against a reliable source.

## 4. Bias and fairness

AI learns from data. If the data reflects historical inequalities, the
system can reproduce or amplify them, for example ranking candidates from
some groups lower, or working less well for some accents or skin tones.
Watch for systematic differences between groups and report them.

## 5. Data protection and confidentiality

- Only enter personal data into AI tools approved for it in the internal AI
  usage policy. The GDPR applies to AI use as to any other processing.
- Do not paste confidential information, trade secrets or credentials into
  public AI tools.
- Remember that prompts and outputs may be stored by the provider.

## 6. When not to trust the output

Stop and verify, or escalate, when:

1. The output affects someone's rights, access to services, money, health or safety.
2. It contradicts what you know, other sources or common sense.
3. The situation is unusual or differs from what the system was designed for.
4. You cannot explain the result to the person it affects.
5. The system behaves differently from before (possible change or malfunction).

## 7. Our internal AI usage policy

Read the current internal AI usage policy (your coordinator records each
issued version in the evidence register). In short:

- Use only approved AI tools for work, for the purposes they are approved for.
- You remain responsible for any work product you create with AI.
- Label AI-generated content where the policy or Art. 50 requires it.
- Report incidents, errors and suspected bias to the AI contact point.

## 8. Where to get help

Contact your AI literacy coordinator or the AI contact point named in the
internal AI usage policy.
