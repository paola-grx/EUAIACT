# Role module: human oversight (Art. 14)

For people assigned to oversee an AI system, or who take decisions based on
the output of a high-risk AI system.

## 1. What oversight is for

Human oversight aims to prevent or minimise risks to health, safety and
fundamental rights. For high-risk systems, Art. 14 requires that overseers
can:

- properly understand the system's capacities and limitations and monitor
  its operation, including detecting anomalies and malfunctions;
- remain aware of **automation bias**;
- correctly interpret the output;
- decide not to use the system, or disregard, override or reverse its output;
- intervene or interrupt the system (a "stop" procedure).

## 2. Automation bias

People tend to accept automated outputs, especially under time pressure or
when the system is usually right. Counter-measures:

- Form your own view on key facts before looking at the recommendation
  where practical.
- Pay extra attention to cases that are unusual, borderline or high-impact.
- Track how often you override. Never overriding can be a warning sign.

## 3. Intervening and overriding

Know **where** the override and stop functions are, **who** you inform, and
**how** you record the decision and your reasons. Practise the stop
procedure at least once.

## 4. Monitoring and reporting

Watch for drift (outputs changing over time), errors concentrated in
certain groups, and complaints. Report serious incidents and malfunctions
through the internal procedure without delay.
